# Win salons on trust, calm design, and clear fees

**Bottom line:** The best way to stand out is not language or price alone. Newer nail POS brands already pitch Vietnamese and low prices. We stand out by being the one system owners and techs **trust about money**: every tip and card fee shown on every ticket, a payout sheet with the check/cash split (no rival we found offers one), **one flat price with all staff included**, no contract, and the salon owns its data and hardware. Wrap that in a calm, Apple-like design built for senior staff. Recommended pricing: **$49/month per salon, all staff and devices included** (free 30 days, then $39/month for 3 months in the pilot). Pass card fees through **at Stripe's cost with no markup** at launch: 2.7% + 5¢ on a reader, which comes out a little under Square's 2.6% + 15¢ on a typical $55 ticket. Add a margin later only once Stripe gives us volume pricing. Keep **two plans at most**, plus a $15 texting add-on. One big risk: card fees cost the salon about **10–20x the software fee**, and our QR / pay-link checkout costs salons about 20% more than a card reader. So getting salons onto a reader early is a pricing decision, not just a hardware one. Elang should own pilot recruiting in Vietnamese networks, a fee-comparison sales kit, free switching help, and the legal checklist: surcharge rules by state, texting registration, California pay rules, and terms and privacy.

> **How to read the numbers.** The research session could not open most vendor pricing pages, so nearly every competitor price comes from third-party blogs, many of them written by rival vendors. Labels used below: **[unverified]** = secondary source, check with the vendor before quoting; **[estimate]** = our own model; **[verified]** = vendor docs or official source.

  - [ABC Salon System](https://abcsalonsystem.com/en/) (MAC Marketing, Vietnamese): $40/month software-only "Full Features", unlimited staff, texts 2¢ each; hardware bundles PRO-X3 $288, PRO-X4 $380, PROMAX-5 $475/month (PRO-X3 promo $160–170/month for year 1); 30-day free trial. **Generic POS (Square, Clover, SpotOn)**
- Square is the number owners compare against: free plan at 2.6% + 15¢ in person, Plus at $49 per location ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/small-business/square-fees)) **[unverified]**.
- Square "leaves the splitting logic to you" and has no turn queue ([Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/best-nail-salon-pos-systems)). - Clover lock-in: devices can't be moved to another processor, reseller contracts run 3 years, and termination fees run $200 to over $1,000 ([Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/blog/what-it-costs-to-leave-your-pos); [KORONA](https://koronapos.com/blog/pos-early-termination-fees/)).

**Where we win (job to get done: "pay my techs fairly with no fights, and never get trapped")**
- **Trusted tech pay:** per-ticket tip and card fee, the check/cash payout split, multi-tech tip split, and techs print their own earnings by PIN. No source shows a rival doing the check/cash split.
- **Tip records for "No Tax on Tips":** nail techs are on the IRS qualified tipped-jobs list (final rules Apr 10, 2026; up to $25k/year) ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/node/153776); [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/04/10/irs-issues-final-regs-on-occupations-eligible-for-obbba-tips-deduction/181462/)) **[verified]**. Clean per-tech tip records are a fresh sales hook.
- **No traps:** no contract, no setup fee, no leased hardware, no new-client cut, and a written promise that the salon can export its data any time. Switching guides show that losing the client list is the main fear ([Hackceleration](https://hackceleration.com/labs/switch/fresha)).
- **Reliability you can demo:** offline mode is already claimed by Fastboy ([Capterra AU](https://www.capterra.com.au/software/1095600/Go-Checkin-POS)), so it is expected, not unique. Prove it live: pull the Wi-Fi mid-checkout.
- **Design for senior staff:** this is the gap between the polished tools (GlossGenius) and the nail tools (registers with check-in screens).
- **Don't compete on:** marketplace or discovery (Fresha, Booksy), hardware (Square, Clover), or feature count.

## $49 flat with card fees at cost beats every rival's real bill

**Why the card fee matters more than the subscription [estimate]**
- Model mid salon: 8 techs, $40k sales, $32.2k card volume, ~585 card tickets per month. Assumes 70% card share, a guess to measure in the pilot.
  - Square Free in person: ~$925/month in fees
  - Stripe reader at list (2.7% + 5¢): ~$899
  - Stripe QR / pay link (2.9% + 30¢): ~$1,109
- Stripe list rates: 2.9% + 30¢ online, 2.7% + 5¢ on Terminal, +10¢ for Tap to Pay ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing?Sid=1); [Flexprice](https://flexprice.io/blog/stripe-pricing-breakdown-2026)) **[unverified, re-check on stripe.com]**.
- On a $55 ticket: Square ≈ 2.87%, Stripe reader ≈ 2.79%, Stripe QR ≈ 3.45% **[estimate]**. So **reader at cost is the only setup where we beat Square on rate.** QR-only loses.
- Our own markup is thin at list rates. If we handle pricing on Stripe Connect, we pay $2 per active salon per month plus 0.25% + 25¢ per payout ([Stripe Connect](https://stripe.com/connect/pricing)). Charging 2.9% + 10¢ would leave just ~$2–22 per salon per month **[estimate]**.

**How we earn more later:** once total volume is large, ask Stripe for custom or interchange-plus pricing. Then offer one flat all-in rate, target 2.6% + 15¢ in person, and keep the gap between that and our real cost ([Stripe platform pricing tool](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/platform-pricing-tools) **[verified]**). Revisit at ~50 salons. The volume Stripe needs for discounts is unknown.

**Small platform fee as a fallback:** if subscription revenue alone is too thin, add **0.2%** (not 0.3%) on card sales, with **no fee on tips**. At a mid salon 0.3% would add ~$97/month for us **[estimate]**, but it puts us ~0.3 points above Square, which is a sales objection.

**Do not:** sell "free POS if you process with us." It ties our income to card share in cash-heavy salons and clashes with "card fee upfront."

**Cash discount / surcharge setting:** off by default, credit cards only (never debit), limited by state. See the legal list below. Cash-discount programs are common in this market, and Mangomint argues they work like a surcharge on clients ([Mangomint](https://mangomint.com/blog/is-cash-discounting-a-scam)).

## Calm, fast, solid: design and motion rules for senior staff

**Look (borrow from Apple, skip the glass)**
- One big number or one main action per screen. Lots of white space, one accent color, soft cards with a light shadow.
- **No see-through glass behind text, prices, names or PINs.** iOS 26 Liquid Glass drew wide legibility complaints ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/09/17/ios-26-liquid-glass-critiques/); [NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass)), and Apple added a more opaque "Tinted" option in iOS 26.1 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/20/apple-will-let-users-roll-back-the-liquid-glass-look-with-new-tinted-option)). Any glass hint goes only on non-text chrome, at 90%+ opacity.
- **Fewer taps beats prettier.** Square's 2025 redesign was meant to declutter, but users called it "awful" because it added taps ([Square Community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Hardware-Setup-Troubleshooting/New-Square-POS-App-interface-is-awful/td-p/663234)). Count taps per checkout as a design metric.

**Sizes for older hands and eyes**
- Research basis: adults 55+ did best with ~32 px targets for tapping and ~64 px for drag or swipe tasks ([UMBC study](https://mdsoar.org/handle/11603/19868)). Older adults make more "slip" errors, where the finger slides off before release ([McGill](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/6108vh322)). WCAG AA minimum is 24 px ([spec](https://specification.website/spec/accessibility/touch-target-size.md)).
- Our spec **[estimate]**:
  - Main buttons (Charge, Next client) at least 64 px tall; anything tappable at least 48 px; at least 8 px between targets
  - PIN keys ~72–80 px
  - Actions fire on finger-up, with a hit area larger than the visual
  - Every swipe action also has a button
- Text: body 18 px on iPad and phone, 16 px on desktop, money totals 40–64 px, nothing under 14 px. Contrast 4.5:1 minimum, aim for 7:1 on money and PIN screens.
- Money safety: a full-screen confirm only for things that can't be undone, naming the amount and person ("Refund $45 to Lan?"). Everything else acts at once with a 5–8 s Undo.

**Vietnamese type**
- Use **Be Vietnam Pro**, built by Vietnamese designers for diacritics ([Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/be-vietnam-pro/about)). Self-host it with the Vietnamese subset so it works offline. Inter drops Vietnamese glyphs if the subset is missing ([Bricks forum](https://forum.bricksbuilder.io/t/duplicate-local-fonts-missing-vietnamese-glyphs-due-to-ignored-google-fonts-language-subsets/37575)).
- Line height: body 1.5, headings ~1.25. Vietnamese stacked marks need more room than English ([TypeDrawers](https://typedrawers.com/discussion/comment/32690)). Use tabular numbers for money.
- Leave ~30% extra button width for Vietnamese labels, and allow two lines.

**Motion ("a little lifelike")**
- Apple springs use two numbers, duration and bounce. Bounce 0 is the default, ~0.15 feels "alive," and ~0.3 is clearly bouncy ([WWDC23 notes](https://wwdcnotes.com/notes/wwdc23/10158)). **Cap ours at 0.15, never above 0.2.**
- Durations: ~100 ms for feedback, 200–300 ms for sheets and modals, 400 ms only for big moves. Animations are usually too long, not too short ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration)).
- Web tools are ready:
  - CSS `linear()` draws real spring curves ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/css-linear-easing-function))
  - Same-page View Transitions are now in all major browsers ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-10-2025))
  - Animate only transform and opacity, so motion stays smooth on cheap iPads even during sync ([Motion docs](https://motion.dev/docs/performance))
- Must support reduced motion: swap slides for quick fades and keep color and check-mark feedback ([W3C](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions)).

| Moment | Motion [estimate, test in pilot] |
|---|---|
| Button press | Shrinks to 97%, springs back (100 / 200 ms) |
| Payment success | Green check draws in, amount stays big; auto-advance after ~1.5 s, tap to skip |
| Receipt sent | Button turns into a check + "Sent", no pop-up (300 ms) |
| Saved offline | Calm amber cloud: "Đã lưu — sẽ đồng bộ / Saved, will sync"; turns green when synced |
| Turn list reorder | Rows slide to new spots, moved row tinted for 1 s (300 ms) |
| Payout totals | Count up from the old value, max 800 ms |
| Wrong PIN | Small shake + red dots (reduced motion: red flash only) |

**Hard rules:** motion never blocks the next tap (the action happens first). One moving thing at a time. Same action, same motion everywhere. Status always has color, icon and words, never motion alone. No splash screens, no forced success screens, no motion over 24 px or scale beyond 0.97–1.05.

## Elang's jobs: get pilots, sell the fee story, keep it legal

**Go-to-market and ads**
- Owners decide by word of mouth, Vietnamese Facebook groups and Zalo, not Capterra ([Hashmeta](https://hashmeta.com/blog/social-media-landscape-vietnam-complete-guide-for-marketers)). There is little public review data from Vietnamese owners, so the pilot is the research.
- Target size **[estimate]**: about 50k–130k US nail salons, roughly 40–51% Vietnamese-run, so about 25–60k target salons ([OCBJ](https://www.ocbj.com/retail/posh-nail-salons-competing-discounters-recovering/); [Matador, old 2015 data](https://matadornetwork.com/life/why-vietnamese-americans-rule-the-nail-salon-scene)). In California ~80% are Vietnamese-run ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/politics/2025/07/workers-nail-salons-labor-bill/)), so start there.
- Channels to test, cheapest first (none proven by sources):
  - Personal network for 2–3 pilots
  - Ask each happy owner for 2 introductions
  - Short Vietnamese videos in FB owner groups (payout sheet, 30-second checkout)
  - A Zalo group for pilot support
  - QR flyers and a referral fee at Vietnamese nail supply stores
  - Ads on Vietnamese nail job apps such as Viet Nails and iNails ([Viet Nails](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6444178458))
  - Trade shows and Little Saigon media later
- Headline to test: "Pay your techs fairly, in seconds — in Vietnamese. $49, everyone included, no contract."

**Sales**
- **Fee calculator** for every visit: put the owner's current monthly statement next to ours, including setup fees, leases and termination fees.
- Ask every owner:
  - What rate they pay
  - Whether they run a cash discount program
  - Their contract end date and exit fee
  - How they pay techs today (an open question in CLAUDE.md)
- "Bring your contract, we'll check your exit cost" service.
- Written promise: no contract, data export any time, the reader is yours.

**Operations**
- **Free switching done for them:** import client lists from CSV or a photo of the paper book, and set up the menu and staff.
- Vietnamese phone and Zalo support. Train the front desk on-site in one visit.
- Help each salon order and set up a card reader at onboarding (it cuts card fees ~20% vs QR).
- Track pilot proof points: fewer pay disputes, minutes to close the day, card fee share, taps per checkout.

**Legal checklist** (all need a lawyer; sources conflict on several points)
- **Surcharge / cash discount:**
  - Banned: CT and MA; ME disputed
  - Capped at 2%: CO and OK
  - CA requires all-in pricing (SB 478)
  - Debit cards can never be surcharged
  - The 2026 Visa/Mastercard settlement is not final
  - Sources: [NFIB](https://www.nfib.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Credit-Card-Surcharging-Guide-Final-PDF.pdf); [Visa](https://corporate.visa.com/en/sites/visa-perspectives/company-news/visa-statement-mdl-settlement.html)
- **Texting:** register A2P 10DLC for each salon (brand ~$4.50, campaign vetting $15, monthly campaign fee $1.50–10) ([Twilio](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260803965530)). Honor opt-outs within 10 business days ([FCC](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-312A1_Rcd.pdf)). Get written consent for promo texts. Check Texas SB 140 registration for marketing texts ([BrandedAgency](https://brandedagency.com/blog/tcpa-sms-compliance)). Find out whether we can register as an ISV (one setup covering all salons).
- **California pay:**
  - Piece-rate and commission staff must be paid separately for rest time, shown on the wage statement (AB 1513 / Labor Code 226.2) ([CA DIR](https://dir.ca.gov/pieceratebackpayelection/AB_1513_FAQs.htm))
  - Manicurist contractor exemption (AB 1514, signed Oct 2025): techs set their own rates, take payment directly, hold a business license, keep their own client list ([DIR](https://dir.ca.gov/chswc/Meetings/2025/12-11-2025-Item-14a-Legislative-Update.pdf))
  - Sunset date unclear, 2029 vs 2031
  - Fines are real: a Temecula salon was cited $1.2M in 2018 ([CA DIR](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2018/2018-65.pdf))
- **Tips:** only voluntary tips count for No Tax on Tips, not mandatory service charges ([RSM](https://www.rsmus.com/insights/services/business-tax/no-tax-on-tips-rules)).
- **Our own papers:** Terms of Service, data processing agreement, privacy policy (check CCPA/CPRA thresholds), Stripe Connect platform terms, Apple Tap to Pay terms for each salon later ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/terminal/payments/setup-reader/tap-to-pay?platform=ios)), Vietnamese versions of key terms, and record retention (payroll 3–4 years; check).
- **Watch:** the FTC and 8 states sued Toast over hidden POS fees ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/why-the-ftc-and-8-states-are-suing-payments-fintech-toast)). "Nothing taken silently" is a compliance shield as well as a pitch.

## Conclusion

The research changes the frame. **This market is really a payments market dressed as a software market.** Incumbents sell "cheap" software and earn on hidden processing, leases and lock-in. So a lower subscription is not our strongest weapon. Showing the owner and techs every dollar is. That makes fee transparency, the check/cash payout sheet and data freedom the product itself, not side features, and it puts the card reader decision on the critical path for pricing. It also reframes "Apple-like": for senior staff, premium means fewer taps, big calm numbers and motion that confirms money moved. Glass and flourish don't make it premium. The biggest unknowns, card vs cash share, what owners pay today, and how they pay techs, can only be answered in the pilot. Those answers should set the final price and the platform fee decision.
