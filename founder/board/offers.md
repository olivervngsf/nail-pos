# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market is easy to reach.** About 25–60k Vietnamese-run salons. Owners decide by word of mouth, Facebook groups and Zalo, and the team speaks their language.
- **The pain is real and has a clear villain.** Rivals hide their money in hardware leases, card markups, contracts and surprise charges. Techs do not trust how turns, tips and fees are worked out.
- **Low effort for the buyer.** 1-hour setup day, phone number + PIN, works offline, no contract, all staff included.
- **Many parts of the stack are cheap for us and worth a lot to the buyer.** Tip + fee on every ticket, two-tech tip split, check/cash payout split, techs print their own pay, honest reviews, tip records for the "No Tax on Tips" deduction.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proof, so buyers will not believe it works.** 0 paying salons, 0 signed pilots, 1–2 interviews. For a buyer the hardest part of the offer is "will this work in my salon?", and today there is nothing to show them.
2. **It sells on price.** "$887 vs $2,400" is the main line. New rivals already pitch Vietnamese + a low price. If the only difference is being cheaper, a rival copies the price and the offer is gone.
3. **Switching is a big sacrifice for the buyer.** A new POS means retraining senior staff, moving client lists and gift cards, and maybe exit fees. The offer does nothing to carry that risk for the owner yet: there is no guarantee and no "we move everything for you".

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which 3 named salons will pilot, and when?
- What does a model salon pay today in total (software, lease, card fees, marketing)? What does it save in real dollars on PolishOS?
- Do techs pick a salon based on fair pay? Can "fair pay" bring an owner better techs or keep them longer?
- What guarantee can you afford? For example: "If you save less than $X in 90 days, we pay back your reader."
- Will you move a salon's data for them and pay its exit fees?
- Could you charge $99 and still be the obvious choice? If not, why not?
- How fast does an owner see the first result: day 1, week 1 or month 1?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Sign 2–3 pilot salons and get real numbers before launch: money saved, tip complaints before vs after, staff using it in week 1.
- Rewrite the offer around the outcome ("techs trust their pay, you keep more money"), not the price. Give it a clear name.
- Add a real guarantee and a "we switch you over" service (data move, staff training on setup day).
- Use real urgency only, for example a limited number of pilot spots that Elang can actually set up each month.
- Test a higher price or a second plan before you lock in $49.
