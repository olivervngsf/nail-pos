# Vietnamese-American Nail Salon Market: Pain Points and Go-to-Market (late 2026)

Research note, 2026-11-16. Method: web search only. Full-page fetches failed for calmatters.org and labor.ucla.edu (blocked by the session network), so several facts come from search-result summaries of those pages, not the pages themselves. Treat every number as "check before quoting". Older figures are marked [OLD].

## 1. Market size, people, geography, and 2023–2026 trends

### Takeaway
There are roughly 50k–130k US nail salons, depending on who counts and how (the most quoted number is 129,682 [OLD, 2015–16]). Vietnamese Americans run about 40–51% nationally and about 80% in California. BLS counts about 210k employed manicurists in 2024, at a median of $16.66/hr. The big 2023–2026 legal story is California manicurist classification: the AB5 exemption lapsed in 2025 and was revived and extended by AB 1514 (signed Oct 2025). Nobody we found has solid numbers on the labor shortage.

### Cited Findings
**Size and ownership**
- [OLD] Nails Magazine 2015–16 stats: 129,682 nail salons; 51% Vietnamese, 40% Caucasian, 5% Black, 3% Hispanic. This comes via secondary articles, not the Big Book itself. — [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/life/why-vietnamese-americans-rule-the-nail-salon-scene); [UCLA AASC policy report](https://www.aasc.ucla.edu/resources/policyreports/Reframing_Immigration_Debate/Reframing_Vietnamese_Ownded_Manicure.pdf)
- [OLD] NAILS 2016–17 Big Book: about $8.53B in 2016 nail-service spending, about 393,581 active nail licenses, and 69,738 nail salons. This conflicts with the 129,682 figure because the two use different counting methods. — [NAILS Big Book (epub)](https://nailsmag.epubxp.com/i/753141/15)
- [OLD] NAILS 2013–14 put the market at $8.28B, while Census put it at about $1.6B/yr, a big gap between trade and government numbers. — [NAILS Big Book (epub)](https://nailsmag.epubxp.com/i/331871/1)
- [OLD] NAILS 2012–13: Vietnamese Americans were 48% of nail professionals. — [OCBJ](https://www.ocbj.com/?p=28543)
- NAILS says Vietnamese-owned salons are about 40% of the US nail market, and it also says no one has precise counts. — [OCBJ](https://www.ocbj.com/retail/posh-nail-salons-competing-discounters-recovering/)
- California: about 80% of nail salons are operated by Vietnamese immigrants, per UCLA Labor Center and California Healthy Nail Salon Collaborative (CHNSC) figures cited at a July 2025 Senate hearing and in CalMatters. More than 80% of CA nail workers are immigrants, mostly Vietnamese women. — [CalMatters, Jul 2025](https://calmatters.org/politics/2025/07/workers-nail-salons-labor-bill/); [LAist](https://laist.com/news/california-nail-salons-minimum-wage-ucla-healthy-collaborative)
- 2024 market estimate: US nail salon market of about $8.8B (Global Market Insights, a paid-report vendor, so lower confidence). — [GMI](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/nail-salon-market/amp)

**Workers and pay**
- BLS: about 210,100 manicurist and pedicurist jobs in 2024; median wage $16.66/hr (May 2024); 71% work in personal care services; growth projected at +7% from 2024 to 2034 (about 14,700 jobs). BLS likely undercounts self-employed and contractor techs. — [BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/manicurists-and-pedicurists.htm)
- Data USA: 382k workers in "nail salons and other personal care services" (a broader category), average salary $32,346 in 2024. — [Data USA](https://datausa.io/profile/naics/nail-salons-other-personal-care-services?redirect=true)
- UCLA/CHNSC March 2024 California study: many CA nail workers are paid by the manicure (piece rate); the workforce is described as fast-growing and facing low wages and misclassification. — [UCLA Labor Center press advisory](https://labor.ucla.edu/press-advisory/californias-fast-growing-nail-salon-workforce-faces-low-wages-misclassification-says-new-research-from-ucla-chnsc); [ABC7](https://abc7.com/nail-salon-industry-ucla-labor-center-study-poor-working-conditions-low-pay/14547140/)
- [OLD, 2018] UCLA "Nail Files" was the first national study of nail salon labor conditions. — [UCLA Nail Files PDF](https://labor.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/NAILFILES_FINAL.pdf)

**Legal trends 2023–2026**
- California AB 1514 was chaptered Oct 3, 2025 (Ch. 305, Stats. 2025) and amends Labor Code 2778 and 2783. It extends the licensed-manicurist exemption from the ABC test. The sunset date is unclear: the Senate floor analysis says Jan 1, 2031, while another summary says Jan 1, 2029. To qualify, the manicurist must set their own rates, take payment directly, hold a business license, and keep their own client book. EDD and the Labor Commissioner must report on manicurist misclassification by June 1, 2026. — [CA Digital Democracy (AB1514)](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/CA_202520260AB1514); [DIR CHSWC legislative update Dec 2025](https://dir.ca.gov/chswc/Meetings/2025/12-11-2025-Item-14a-Legislative-Update.pdf); [Senate analysis](https://billtexts.s3.amazonaws.com/ca/ca-analysishttps-leginfo-legislature-ca-gov-faces-billAnalysisClient-xhtml-bill-id-202520260AB1514-ca-analysis-391006.pdf)
- The previous exemption (extended by AB 1561 in 2021) was set to expire Jan 1, 2025, and the Senate analysis calls it "previously expired". That suggests manicurists fell under the ABC test for part of 2025. — [Buchalter, Dec 2024](https://www.buchalter.com/insights/beauty-is-pain-lessons-and-trends-impacting-the-beauty-industry-and-employment-law-concerns-for-2025-and-beyond/)
- The AB 1514 debate was framed by some as targeting Vietnamese salons, and labor groups were split. — [Soar summary](https://cp.soar.com/articles/5347834/California/California-bill-AB-15-14-faces-scrutiny-for-targeting-Vietnamese-nail-salons)
- Enforcement examples: Young's Nail Spa in Temecula, CA was cited for more than $1.2M for misclassifying and underpaying 36 workers, with no hourly pay and no overtime for weeks up to 50 hours [OLD, 2018]. — [CA DIR press release](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2018/2018-65.pdf)
- The NY Attorney General recovered $300k for more than 100 workers at Envy Nails (25 salons) in 2023; the case included a criminal conviction. — [NY AG](https://ag.ny.gov/index%2Ephp/press-release/2023/attorney-general-james-recovers-300000-unpaid-wages-new-york-city-nail-salon)
- A Rhode Island salon owner was ordered to pay $753,500 in back wages and damages (date not confirmed). — [AOL/news](https://www.aol.com/callous-indifference-nail-salon-owner-093026231.html)
- Federal "No Tax on Tips" (OBBBA): manicurists and pedicurists (nail technicians) are on the IRS qualified tipped occupations list (TTOC code 605). Final regulations came out Apr 10, 2026. The deduction covers up to $25k/yr in qualified tips for tax years after Dec 31, 2024. Service charges don't count unless the customer can change them. — [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/node/153776); [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/04/10/irs-issues-final-regs-on-occupations-eligible-for-obbba-tips-deduction/181462/); [RSM](https://www.rsmus.com/insights/services/business-tax/no-tax-on-tips-rules)

### Inferences
- A practical sizing frame: about 50–130k salons, and if about 40–50% are Vietnamese-run, that is about 25–60k target salons. California is the densest market, followed (by common knowledge, not verified here) by TX, FL, GA, and the NY/NJ area.
- "No Tax on Tips" makes clean, per-tech tip records valuable from 2025 on. Techs need documented tips to claim the deduction. Use this as a sales hook: "your techs' tips, recorded properly."
- California salons now face two paths. Either they meet the AB 1514 contractor conditions (techs set their own prices, take their own payments, hold a business license), or they treat techs as employees under AB 1513 piece-rate rules. Both paths need better records than paper.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) NAILS Big Book figures were found. Buy or request the latest edition.
- No state-by-state salon counts were found. Census County Business Patterns (NAICS 812113) would answer this.
- No hard data on the labor shortage or the 2023–2026 trend in salon openings and closures.
- The exact AB 1514 sunset date (2029 vs 2031) wasn't confirmed. Check the chaptered text on leginfo.ca.gov.
- No 2025–2026 UCLA/CHNSC report by name was found.

## 2. Owner and tech pain points with current software and practices

### Takeaway
The pain is mostly about money and trust: commission and tip math, the check/cash split, card fees taken from tips, and slow card-tip payouts. On top of that come lock-in contracts and leased "free" terminals, plus fees charged on top (Fresha's 20% new-client cut). There is very little public review data from Vietnamese owners themselves. They talk in Facebook and Zalo groups, not on Capterra.

### Cited Findings
- Commission splits typically run from 40/60 to 60/40, with 50/50 the most common (vendor guides, medium confidence). — [Zenoti blog](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/how-do-nail-salons-pay-their-employees); [Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/how-much-do-nail-techs-make)
- Salons may take a percentage of card tips for processing; one owner quotes merchant fees of 1.5–2.5% or more. Card tips run through payroll can arrive days or weeks late and have tax withheld, while cash tips reach the tech the same day. — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/finance/learn/how-much-to-tip-nail-salon); [Nailpro](https://www.nailpro.com/the-ins-and-outs-of-accepting-gratuities/)
- Square forum users struggle to split one card transaction, including the tip, across several techs, a common nail-salon case (one client, two techs). — [Square Community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Staff-Payroll/How-to-split-credit-card-transactions-between-employees/td-p/835610)
- California law (AB 1513 / Labor Code 226.2) requires piece-rate and commission employees to get separate pay for rest periods and nonproductive time (the higher of minimum wage or their average hourly rate), with those hours itemized on the wage statement. The Board of Barbering & Cosmetology says employers can't pay only a flat commission or percentage with no base wage. — [CA DIR FAQ](https://dir.ca.gov/pieceratebackpayelection/AB_1513_FAQs.htm); [BBC brochure](https://barbercosmo.ca.gov/forms_pubs/publications/bbc_workers_rights_brochure_licensees.pdf)
- POS contracts: "free" terminals are often leases (for example $50–100/mo for a $500 device). Early termination fees run $200 to more than $1,000, and some contracts charge all remaining fees through liquidated damages. Clover resellers charge about $295–595 with 3-year terms and auto-renewal; one reported Shift4/SkyTab case reached $45,000. Clover devices can't be moved to another processor. — [KORONA POS](https://koronapos.com/blog/pos-early-termination-fees/); [Merchant Maverick](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/liquidated-damages-expensive-early-termination-fee-ever); [Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/blog/what-it-costs-to-leave-your-pos)
- Fresha charges a one-time 20% commission on a new marketplace client's first booking ($6 US minimum). Plans are listed at about $19.95/mo for independents and $14.95 per member on teams (third-party figures). — [Fresha pricing](https://fresha.com/pricing); [GlossGenius](https://glossgenius.com/blog/fresha-pricing); [Costbench](https://www.costbench.com/software/salon-spa/fresha/)
- Square Appointments has no new-client fee and is priced per location (Plus about $49, Premium about $149 per location; sources conflict). — [Square vs Fresha](https://squareup.com/au/en/compare/square-vs-fresha); [Costbench](https://costbench.com/compare/fresha-vs-square-appointments/)
- Nail-specific incumbents: Zota is described as having a "strong presence in high-volume Vietnamese-owned nail salons", but as not all-in-one, with only partial online booking (this comes from Zenoti, a competitor). — [Zenoti comparison](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/best-nail-salon-pos-systems)
- POSNailStore has one Capterra review, rated 1/5 on overall, ease of use, and support. One review proves little. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/135633/POSNailStore/)
- Vagaro complaints cited by GlossGenius (a competitor): weak QuickBooks integration and poor customer service. — [GlossGenius](https://webflow.glossgenius.com/blog/salon-pos-system)
- Vietnamese-language software in this space (iNails, Viet Nails apps) focuses on job posts for hiring techs and listings of salons for sale. iNails says it can search all US nail salons. — [Viet Nails app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6444178458); [iNails](https://apps.apple.com/vn/app/inails/id1120989444)

### Inferences
- The strongest wedge is a payout sheet techs trust: per-tech commission, tips, any card fee shown on the ticket, and the check/cash split. Disputes come from math that can't be seen. Showing it on every ticket and every payout addresses both tech trust and owner record-keeping.
- Contract fear is a selling point: "no contract, no leased hardware, export your data any time."
- Multi-tech tickets (tip split across techs) are a known gap in general tools like Square. Make it first-class.
- Turn fairness, walk-outs, and no-shows came up in no sources found here. They are likely real (per CLAUDE.md research) but need validation in pilot interviews.

### Gaps
- No Reddit or Facebook first-hand threads were retrieved (search did not surface them, and fetching was blocked). Mine r/Nails, r/NailTechs, and Vietnamese FB groups ("Hội chủ tiệm nail", "Thợ Nail Mỹ") by hand.
- No current published pricing for nail-specific incumbents (Zota, Mango for Salon, NailSoft, POSNailStore) was found. These are quote-based.
- No data on chargeback rates or IRS audit frequency for nail salons.

## 3. How owners choose and switch POS (channels and barriers)

### Takeaway
Very little written evidence exists online. Vietnamese salon owners decide through word of mouth, Vietnamese-language Facebook groups, Zalo, and in-person trust (distributors, other owners). Switching barriers are contract or lease lock-in, processor-locked hardware, and fear of losing the client list.

### Cited Findings
- Among Vietnamese users, Facebook Groups are where people discover, discuss, and buy (Vietnam-market data). Zalo reaches users less active on Facebook. — [Hashmeta](https://hashmeta.com/blog/social-media-landscape-vietnam-complete-guide-for-marketers); [Elite Asia 2026](https://www.eliteasia.co/top-digital-and-social-media-trends-in-vietnam-in-2026/)
- Vietnamese job and listing apps (iNails, Viet Nails) already reach US salon owners and techs, so they are a possible ad and partner channel. — [Viet Nails app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6444178458)
- NailSoft announced it had become the "official provider of POS system" for an organization, a sign that incumbents use association and partner deals. — [NailSoft news](https://nailsoft.com/nailsoft-news/nailsoft-becomes-official-provider-of-pos-system/)
- Switching cost: processor-locked terminals (Clover can't be repointed), multi-year contracts with auto-renewal, and liquidated damages. — [Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/blog/what-it-costs-to-leave-your-pos); [KORONA](https://koronapos.com/blog/fiserv-clover-lock-in/)
- Fresha-switch guides focus on "how to take your clients with you", so exporting the client list is the core switching fear. — [Hackceleration](https://hackceleration.com/labs/switch/fresha)

### Inferences (playbook for a two-person team)
- **Elang (sales, ops):** start with 2–3 pilot salons through personal networks. Ask each happy owner for 2 introductions (salons are tight networks). Post short Vietnamese videos in FB owner groups showing the payout sheet and a 30-second checkout. Run a Zalo OA or group for pilot support.
- **Distributors and supply stores:** leave flyers or QR cards at Vietnamese nail supply shops and offer a referral fee. This needs validation; no source confirmed it as a channel.
- **Trade shows:** booths at US Vietnamese nail expos are a later step. No source confirmed which US shows draw Vietnamese owners; check the NAILS calendar, Premiere Orlando, ISSE Long Beach, and Vietnamese-run expos.
- **Vietnamese media:** Người Việt and Viễn Đông in Orange County, VOA/SBTN, and Little Saigon radio are paid options for later; unverified reach.
- **Switching kit:** free import of client lists (CSV or photo of a paper book), month-to-month only, works with any card reader or a QR code (no hardware lock), a "bring your contract, we'll check your exit cost" service, and a written data-export promise.
- **Proof points to collect in the pilot:** fewer pay disputes, minutes saved closing out the day, and the card-fee share techs see.

### Gaps
- No sources on how many Vietnamese owners use which POS, or what drives a switch.
- No verified list of Vietnamese-language Facebook groups or member counts.
- No confirmation that US trade shows (Nail Expo, ISSE) draw Vietnamese salon owners in large numbers.
- Nothing found on Nguoi Viet or Viet Bao coverage of nail POS.

## 4. Legal and compliance checklist for the sales/ops/legal partner

### Takeaway
The main items to handle are: surcharge rules (CT and MA ban surcharges; CA requires all-in pricing; CO and OK cap at 2%), texting (10DLC registration is required, and TCPA opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days), California piece-rate and wage-statement rules plus the AB 1514 contractor conditions, the OBBBA tip records, and standard Stripe Connect platform terms.

### Cited Findings
- Surcharging: Connecticut and Massachusetts ban credit-card surcharges, while cash discounts are allowed; Maine is disputed between sources. California SB 478 (effective Jul 1, 2024) requires the full price up front, so no surprise fee lines. Colorado caps surcharges at 2% (SB 21-091) and Oklahoma at 2%. — [NFIB guide, Jul 2025](https://www.nfib.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Credit-Card-Surcharging-Guide-Final-PDF.pdf); [Cozen O'Connor](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2024/credit-card-surcharging-regulatory-changes-create-both-risks-and-opportunities-for-businesses); [eBizCharge 2025 PDF](https://ebizcharge.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Credit-Card-Surcharge-Laws-by-State-2025.pdf)
- Texting: carriers block unregistered A2P 10DLC traffic since Feb 1, 2025 (vendor claim), and registration goes through The Campaign Registry. — [BrandedAgency](https://brandedagency.com/blog/tcpa-sms-compliance); [Nextiva](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/business-text-messaging-compliance.html)
- FCC consent-revocation rule took effect Apr 11, 2025: opt-outs must be processed within 10 business days, and any reasonable means of opting out counts. The rule that one opt-out stops all of a business's messages was delayed to Apr 11, 2026; its status after that is unconfirmed. — [FCC DA 25-312](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-312A1_Rcd.pdf); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/fcc-issues-one-year-limited-waiver-of-new-tcpa-consent-revocation-rule)
- Texas SB 140 (effective Sep 1, 2025) requires registration and a surety bond for businesses that send marketing texts. — [BrandedAgency](https://brandedagency.com/blog/tcpa-sms-compliance)
- Appointment reminders are generally informational; promo texts need express written consent. — [BrandedAgency](https://brandedagency.com/blog/tcpa-sms-compliance)
- CA piece-rate and commission wage statements must show rest-period hours, the rate, and gross pay (Labor Code 226.2). — [CA DIR](https://dir.ca.gov/pieceratebackpayelection/piecerate.html)
- AB 1514 contractor conditions for manicurists: they set their own rates, take payment directly, hold a business license, and keep their own client list. — [DIR CHSWC update](https://dir.ca.gov/chswc/Meetings/2025/12-11-2025-Item-14a-Legislative-Update.pdf)
- No-tax-on-tips: only voluntary tips qualify, not mandatory service charges. — [RSM](https://www.rsmus.com/insights/services/business-tax/no-tax-on-tips-rules)

### Inferences
- Product: offer a "cash discount" or "card fee shown" setting per state, and default to all-in pricing in CA, CT, and MA. Never deduct card fees from tips silently. Show them on the ticket, which is already a design rule.
- Product: in California, let the owner choose employee mode (hours, rest-period pay, wage-statement fields) or contractor mode (booth rent or the tech's own payment collection). In contractor mode the platform may need to route payments to the tech's own Stripe account to satisfy "takes payment directly". Have a lawyer check this.
- Legal partner tasks: Terms of Service and a data processing agreement; a privacy policy that covers CCPA/CPRA (it may apply once we process data for CA salons, since CCPA covers businesses over its revenue or consumer-count thresholds; verify); Stripe Connect platform agreement (we are a platform, not a payment facilitator, if Stripe holds the merchant relationship); a 10DLC brand and campaign for each salon; SMS consent language; and Vietnamese translations of key terms.

### Gaps
- No source pulled on Stripe Connect platform vs payment facilitator obligations, CCPA thresholds, or record-retention periods (FLSA payroll records 3 years; CA 3–4 years). These come from general knowledge and must be verified.
- Maine surcharge status is unresolved.
- Status of the FCC "revoke-all" rule after Apr 2026 is unknown.

## 5. Differentiation: what would make a salon switch

### Takeaway
Win on trust and simplicity. Lead with fair, visible tech pay (per-ticket tips and fees, a trusted payout sheet, tip records for no-tax-on-tips), a flat price with no contract and no new-client cut, Vietnamese-first staff screens, and switching help. Avoid competing on feature count with Square or Fresha.

### Cited Findings
- Fresha's 20% new-client fee and add-ons priced per location are known irritants, and alternatives market against them. — [Pabau](https://pabau.com/blog/fresha-alternatives/); [Medium "True Cost of Fresha"](https://medium.com/@asbaines/the-true-cost-of-fresha-276d6856bcc0)
- Square pitches "no new-client fee" against Fresha, so flat pricing is table stakes, not unique. — [Square](https://squareup.com/au/en/compare/square-vs-fresha)
- Nail-specific incumbents like Zota are strong in high-volume Vietnamese salons but weaker on all-in-one features such as booking (competitor claim). — [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/best-nail-salon-pos-systems)
- Multi-tech tip splits are a pain point in general POS tools. — [Square Community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Staff-Payroll/How-to-split-credit-card-transactions-between-employees/td-p/835610)
- Legal pressure (AB 1514, AB 1513, wage-theft cases with fines over $1M) raises the cost of bad records. — [CA DIR 2018](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2018/2018-65.pdf)

### Inferences
- Positioning line: "Pay your techs fairly, in seconds — in Vietnamese." Proof: the payout sheet, with tips and fees visible on every ticket.
- Ranked switch reasons to test in the pilot: (1) no pay disputes / a trusted payout sheet; (2) no contract, no hardware lock, a flat price; (3) Vietnamese for staff; (4) a turn list that shows facts; (5) booking and text reminders without the 20% cut; (6) compliance help (CA wage statement fields, tip records).
- Don't compete on marketplace or discovery (Fresha and Booksy) or hardware (Square, Clover). Be the "back office the owner and techs trust."
- Free migration done for them by Elang is a cheap and strong edge for a two-person team.

### Gaps
- No survey data ranks owner switch reasons. This must come from pilot interviews.
- Turn-list fairness and walk-out cost have no sourced figures.
